# Who else is doing this

Researched 2026-10-21 from web searches and the products' own pages. Prices and features change. I have not used any of these apps myself, and the App Store and vendor pages are marketing, so treat the details as claims.

## The closest: Dvash Hebrew

An iOS and iPadOS app by a single developer (Jan Ihmels). Daily short news articles and songs, a library of beginner articles, and pieces on history, people and places.

- Tap a word or sentence for its definition and pronunciation.
- Nikud (vowel marks) can be shown or hidden.
- Articles can be filtered by level.
- Saved words are reviewed with spaced-repetition quizzes.
- Free tier: 3 articles and 1 song a month. Subscriptions at $4.99 and $34.99 (the page does not say for what period).
- App Store rating 4.9 from 19 ratings: very few. iOS only, as far as the listing shows.

Source: [Dvash Hebrew on the App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6503658289).

## Courses and practice

- **Duolingo.** Teaches the alphabet and basics. A competitor's blog (so, biased) says nikud use is inconsistent, there are no stories, and unpointed reading is not covered.
- **Clozemaster.** Sentence practice for intermediate learners.
- **Easy-Hebrew newspapers.** Bereshit (beginners), Yanshuf (intermediate), Sha'ar La'Matchil (weekly) and Ivrit magazine (Jerusalem Post). Written by humans at a learner's level. Not interactive, and I did not check whether each is still published.

## Reference tools

- **Pealim.** Verb and word forms. Paid app.
- **Morfix.** A Hebrew-English dictionary.
- **DICTA Nakdan.** Adds nikud to Hebrew text automatically.

## AI story apps (other languages)

Story Languages, LibroAI, TaleGPT and Readle generate or curate graded stories with tap-to-translate. I did not find that any of them supports Hebrew.

## What this means for us

What others seem not to do, or do not say they do:
- **Show how a text's level is measured.** LingQ says it finds content "just above your level". Nobody explains how. We measure it and say how.
- **Show a word's root and binyan when you tap it.** I found no competitor that says so.
- **Put the right English word over each Hebrew word of a sentence,** chosen from the whole paragraph. Readlang offers AI context explanations, but only on a paid tier.
- **Work on any phone or laptop, free, with no account.** Dvash is iOS only.
- **A guided path and topics over real texts** that are not simplified or machine-written.

Where we are behind:
- **No true beginner texts.** Dvash and the easy-Hebrew newspapers cover that.
- **No fresh content every day.** Dvash adds news on weekdays.
- **No import of your own text.** LingQ and Readlang have it.
- **No nikud toggle.** Dvash has one. We decided it does not fit our readers, but beginners may ask.
- **Almost no one has used ours yet.**

Open questions worth checking before we lean on any of this: does Readlang still support Hebrew; how does LingQ handle Hebrew prefixes; does Dvash show roots.
